## Purpose

Give an owner a short, concrete plan for reaching the customers a business or offering is for: who to reach, with what message, through which channels, at what cost, and how to tell whether it is working.

## What to produce

One Markdown document with these sections, in this order:

1. **Goal:** what the marketing should achieve in the next three to six months, as a number where possible (new customers, bookings, sales).
2. **Who it is for:** one to three customer groups, each with what they care about and where they already spend attention.
3. **The message:** one sentence on why these customers should choose this offering, and the proof that backs it.
4. **Channels:** for each channel chosen (for example word of mouth, local listings, social media, signage, partnerships, email), what is done, how often, by whom, and what it costs.
5. **Calendar:** the first twelve weeks, week by week or fortnight by fortnight.
6. **Budget:** the total and its split across channels.
7. **Measures:** how each channel's results are counted, and when the plan is reviewed.

## What good looks like

- The channels follow from where the stated customers are, not from what is fashionable.
- Every activity has an owner, a frequency and a cost, even if the cost is only time.
- The budget adds up and matches the channels.
- Results can be counted with what the owner has (a till, a booking system, a simple tally), and the plan says how.
- If a business plan is supplied as context, the customers, prices and budget agree with it.
- Plain words; no marketing jargon the owner would need explained.

## What to ask the owner

- What is being marketed, and to whom, in their own words.
- How much money and time per week they can put into marketing.
- What they have tried already, and what happened.
- Any channels they will not use, or already have (a website, a social media page, a customer list).
- What result in three months would make them call it a success.
