## Purpose

Write down how a recurring task is done so that anyone trained for it gets the same result every time, safely, and knows what to do when something goes wrong. Typical tasks: opening or closing a shop, cleaning and maintaining equipment, handling a refund, taking a delivery.

## What to produce

One Markdown document with these sections, in this order:

1. **Title and scope:** the task, when it is done, and what it does not cover.
2. **Who does it:** the role, and any training or permission they need.
3. **What is needed:** tools, materials, safety equipment and records, as a checklist.
4. **Safety:** the hazards and the precautions, before the first step.
5. **Steps:** numbered, one action each, in the order they are done. Where a step has a check (a reading, a look, a count), say what the right result is.
6. **When something goes wrong:** for each likely problem, what to do and whom to tell.
7. **Records:** what is written down or logged, and where it is kept.
8. **Review:** who keeps this procedure up to date, and how often it is reviewed.

## What good looks like

- Each step is one action a new person could follow without asking, in the words the people doing it use.
- Every check states the right result, so the person knows whether to go on.
- Safety precautions come before the steps they apply to, never after.
- Legal or health requirements are named for the owner to confirm, never stated as settled law.
- Nothing is left to memory that the procedure could say.
- It can be printed and followed on one to three pages.

## What to ask the owner

- Which task, and why it needs a written procedure (new staff, mistakes, an inspection).
- How it is done today, step by step, as they or their best worker would describe it.
- What goes wrong most often, and what has gone badly wrong before.
- Any equipment manuals, rules or inspection requirements that apply.
- Who will use the procedure, and how they will see it (printed, on a phone, on the wall).
