## Purpose

Settle, before work starts, what a project is meant to achieve and the limits it works within, so the owner and everyone involved agree on the same thing and can tell later whether it succeeded.

## What to produce

One Markdown document with these sections, in this order:

1. **Purpose:** why the project exists, in two or three sentences.
2. **Outcomes:** what will be true when it is done, each one checkable.
3. **Scope:** what is included, and, as clearly, what is not.
4. **People:** who decides, who does the work, and who must be kept informed.
5. **Constraints:** budget, deadline, and any fixed choices (a location, a supplier, a standard to meet).
6. **Milestones:** three to six points along the way, each with a rough date.
7. **Risks and assumptions:** what is being taken for granted, and what could stop the project.
8. **How it ends:** who confirms it is done, and how.

## What good looks like

- Every outcome can be checked by someone other than the person who did the work.
- Out of scope is written down, not left implicit.
- One person is named as the one who decides when there is disagreement.
- Dates and budget are the owner's figures or marked as estimates.
- It fits on one or two pages; detail belongs in later documents.

## What to ask the owner

- What the project is, and why now, in their own words.
- What would make them say it went well, and what would make it a failure.
- What it must not include or change.
- The budget, the deadline, and anything already decided.
- Who else is involved, and who has the final say.
