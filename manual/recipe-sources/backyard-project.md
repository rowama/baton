## Here, now {#here-now}

Use **backyard project** for a job around the house or yard: a flower bed, a raised bed, a gravel path, a shed, a pergola. It is Baton used for something that is not software, and it shows how people, not just models, can be the ones doing the work: you or someone you name does the physical job; Baton keeps the plan, the permits and the checks in order.

Tell your assistant what you want: "A raised vegetable bed along the south fence." It is sized, and the process fits the size.

## What you will be asked {#what-you-are-asked}

At the start: a short title, what you want in your own words, and, if you know them, notes about the site (size, sun, slope, access). Then, depending on the size:

| Size | Typical work | You are asked |
|---|---|---|
| **minor** | A flower bed, a few pots, mulching, a border edging. An afternoon or a weekend, nothing structural. | To accept the finished work |
| **standard** | A raised bed, a gravel path, loose pavers, a trellis. Several days; changes the layout; may need hired tools. | To accept the finished work |
| **major** | A shed, pergola, deck, retaining wall, anything with a foundation or near a boundary, or that a permit or neighbours may care about. | To approve the layout, to say the permit is in hand (or not needed), whether the finished structure passed inspection, and to accept it |

Signs it is **major**: footings or a slab, over 2 metres tall, roofed, electrical or water connections, within 1.5 metres of a boundary, or a homeowners' association.

## What it produces {#what-it-produces}

A site survey with photographs, a layout, a check of permits and neighbours (for major work), a list of materials and tools, and a photographed record of the finished work. Photographs are kept with the record in your project.

## What it needs {#what-it-needs}

- A **person** for the hands-on parts. That is you, or someone you name. Baton tells them what is needed and where to put the result; nobody else does it for them.
- A helper that can look at photographs for the survey and layout.
- Helpers that can reason about plans and write them up.

## Example {#example}

> **Note:** You say: "A raised vegetable bed along the south fence, about 2 by 1 metres." It is sized **standard**. A site survey asks for photos and measurements; a layout follows. A materials and tools list comes next, then the work itself, and you accept it with a final photo.

## If it goes wrong {#if-it-goes-wrong}

- **It looks bigger than you thought** (a footing, near a boundary): it will be sized **major**. That is the point of asking; say if you disagree.
- **A permit is needed and you do not have it:** the work waits at the permit check until you can say it is in hand or not needed.
- **The finished work does not pass inspection:** say so; it goes back for the work to be put right.
